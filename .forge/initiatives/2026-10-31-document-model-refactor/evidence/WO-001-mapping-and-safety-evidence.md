# WO-001 — FDX mapping and safety evidence

- **Date:** 2026-10-31.
- **Scope:** Evidence and candidate contracts only. No Rust, native JSON behavior, Cargo manifest, or application behavior changed.
- **Evidence basis:** Current [core model and limits](../../../crates/scriptcraft-core/src/lib.rs#L13-L18), [ID/role definitions](../../../crates/scriptcraft-core/src/lib.rs#L47-L112), [project/profile fields](../../../crates/scriptcraft-core/src/lib.rs#L139-L219), and [editing transitions](../../../crates/scriptcraft-core/src/editing.rs#L381-L408) / [`enter_transition`](../../../crates/scriptcraft-core/src/editing.rs#L827-L836); the two checked-in references [`reference.fdx`](../../../.reference/reference.fdx) and [`reference2.fdx`](../../../.reference/reference2.fdx); the [target blueprint](../../../blueprints/technical-context.md#L43-L49) and user decisions in the initiative; and a local inspection of cached `quick-xml` 0.41.0 package source/manifest. References were parsed for structural counts and names only. No screenplay text, attribute values other than role labels, embedded image bytes, or recorded local paths were emitted or copied.
- **Confidence convention:** `Observed` means present in the supplied file or current code; it does not establish a general FDX schema rule. `Candidate` means plausible but not proven in Final Draft. `Blocker` means no safe mapping or transformation is established by this evidence.

## Fixture observations and limits

Both references declare `FinalDraft` / `Script`, `Version="6"`, and UTF-8. The blank reference has one empty Scene Heading in Content; the populated reference has 34 Content paragraphs, including 17 distinct explicit paragraph type labels and three paragraphs without a Type attribute. Both have 56 title-page paragraphs. The populated reference also contains SceneProperties under headings, per-role ElementSettings, title-page structure, list/board/image records and other top-level sections. These are bounded observations, not schema completeness, producer-version identification, or proof of Final Draft acceptance. In particular, neither reference proves multi-run body formatting, a populated ScriptNote, dual dialogue, actual scene-number encoding, manual page breaks, alternate versions, or the behavior of a subsequent Final Draft save.

The checked-in privacy-safe fixtures beside this report are deliberately synthetic structural test inputs. They use invented IDs and text and contain no copied source image data or path. They are not asserted to be Final Draft-generated, schema-conformant, or accepted by Final Draft. A Python standard-library XML parse was used only to inventory the supplied files; it is not the proposed production parser and is not an XML security or interoperability test.

### Role layout and transition defaults (provisional)

- Preserve per-document FDX ElementSettings for all observed roles; do not replace them with global guesses. Honor a role's source visibility/print settings when a future renderer can represent them; otherwise preserve the source and report a concrete limitation rather than displaying hidden text as ordinary script.
- For existing seven ScriptCraft roles, retain the current fixed-profile geometry and editing behavior pending an explicit, tested new-profile mapping. Current Enter transitions are Scene Heading→Action, Action→Action, Character→Dialogue, Dialogue→Action, Parenthetical→Dialogue, Transition→Scene Heading, Note→Note. Current role cycling and cue uppercasing remain code-specific behavior; imported source text must not be normalized during parse.
- **Agent-default for added roles, not product approval:** make every new role explicit and independently editable; do not silently translate a new role on Enter, Tab, layout, or JSON serialization. Until role-specific transitions and geometry are specified and tested, keep the role unchanged when splitting its own text and avoid cross-role automatic transition; do not make any new role an implicit Action/General fallback. This intentionally conservative default requires editor/layout tests before WO-005 can claim behavior.
- The current `PageProfile` has only seven role boxes. FDX supplies role-specific settings in these samples, but no validated ScriptCraft values. Exact new-role geometry, pagination, printing/visibility and transition matrix are unresolved mapping work, not inferred from names.

## Identity and unsupported-source ownership

Use separate namespaces/domains for the ScriptCraft project/element IDs and source document reference, paragraph, title, nested scene/beat, cast/member, character, list-item, board, image, revision/tag and other IDs. Keep source IDs and source bytes/tree attached to their original section; maintain explicit reference edges only where the source grammar is understood. Do not infer that matching strings across domains are links or that different strings are independent. Validate duplicates and dangling known references before changing a dependent object. Never fetch a recorded image path. Embedded image bytes and internal board/item/image links are source-owned; no payload or path from either supplied reference is present in these evidence fixtures.

Preservation strategies compared:

| Strategy | Advantage | Unresolved safety/compatibility | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard FDX-only mappings | Single file; best chance of staying within documented producer conventions. | No proven standard home for ScriptCraft identity, schema, full page profile, three editing policies, and exact role boxes; unknown fields may be changed by another editor. | Preferred only for fields with verified standard semantics; insufficient as the only app-state plan. |
| Private FDX extension | Co-locates app state and source file. | No namespace/schema selected or tested; Final Draft and other editors may strip, rewrite, reject, or ignore it. Not evidence of compatibility. | Candidate for later explicit review; not approved or relied on. |
| Sidecar | Can store ScriptCraft-only state without guessing standard FDX meanings. | Rename/copy/missing-sidecar behavior, atomic two-file replacement, discovery, backup and external editor behavior are unresolved; may separate required state from the FDX. | Candidate only; not selected and not a safe established home. |
| Reconstruct from defaults/visible text | Simple implementation. | Loses or alters identity, profile, policy, title structure, formatting and opaque source content. | Rejected. |

**Mapping blocker for dependent native-model work:** No durable, tested home is established for project identity/schema, complete deterministic profile inputs and geometry, and edit-policy fields. The evidence shows plausible FDX fields for some dimensions/fonts, but not a reversible mapping. Neither private extension nor sidecar has an approved/tested preservation contract. WO-002/codec design must not treat these as solved; obtain a safe durable-state decision or demonstrate a standard mapping with fixtures before promising native save/reopen. This finding is not a request to alter product policy or implement an extension in WO-001.

## XML library and security assessment

- **Candidate inspected:** cached `quick-xml` 0.41.0 package metadata declares MIT; its `NsReader` resolves namespace bindings, and its event reader/writer exposes ordered events and retains event data suitable for a caller-managed source representation. The current core has no direct XML dependency. The lockfile contains quick-xml entries for other packages, but `cargo tree --workspace -i quick-xml@0.41.0` and `...@0.30.0` on this host found no active workspace dependency edge. This inspection is not a dependency selection, current-release survey, security audit, or proof of suitability.
- **Potential fit:** namespace-aware event reading can distinguish expanded names while the caller retains source ordering and opaque events. MIT is compatible with the workspace's MIT license. `quick-xml` is not a DOM/reconciliation layer; preserving event order, comments, declarations, namespace context and unknown mixed children would remain ScriptCraft responsibilities.
- **Security boundary:** reject any `DocType` before accepting document content; do not process external general/parameter entities, resolve URLs or paths, or expand arbitrary declarations. Treat general references other than predefined/numeric references as unsupported unless explicitly bounded and verified. Enforce input bytes before parsing and counters for depth, event/node count, per-element attributes, name/value sizes, total text, and embedded image bytes before allocating or retaining. Enforce UTF-8 explicitly. Test malformed XML, deep nesting, oversized names/attributes/text, duplicate IDs, namespace floods, DTD/entity payloads, and oversized embedded images with no external I/O. Library namespace/event APIs do not alone establish these safety properties.
- **Resource budget proposal (not yet parser-tested):** configurable caps of 16 MiB total source bytes, 100,000 elements/events retained, depth 128, 128 attributes per element, 64 KiB per name/attribute, 8 MiB aggregate text, and 8 MiB aggregate embedded-image bytes (4 MiB per image). Reject on first exceed; never truncate. These are conservative initial engineering limits, not observed Final Draft maxima or approved universal support limits. WO-006 must test boundary, boundary+1, and aggregate interaction cases against the actual parser and privacy-safe generated inputs; revise before adoption if evidence warrants. No production parser or resource-limit test was run in WO-001.
- **Encoding proposal:** accept UTF-8 with optional UTF-8 BOM; require any XML encoding declaration to agree, reject other encodings and invalid XML characters without replacement, and write generated data as UTF-8. Both supplied files declare UTF-8 only. This is an agent-default candidate, not evidence that all Version 6 files use UTF-8; obtain permission-safe alternate-encoding fixtures before broad compatibility claims.
- **Lexical preservation:** event parsing/writing does not promise byte identity for attribute order/spacing, quote style, entity spelling, line endings, CDATA, prefixes, declaration, comments, or whitespace. A namespace-aware semantic tree may normalize these too. No-op byte identity and edited-file semantic retention are separate contracts; retain original source bytes independently of path and do not claim untouched lexical identity until an actual implementation test proves it. Unknown content whose namespace/anchor/reference cannot be preserved safely must cause a safe stop, not silent discard.
- **Unsafe code:** ScriptCraft core has `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]` and the workspace denies unsafe code. The chosen parser must not require unsafe calls from ScriptCraft. This crate policy is not a substitute for auditing the dependency's source/release, dependencies and advisories at the time a direct dependency is proposed.

## Unsafe-edit matrix

| Operation | Safe only if | Otherwise |
|---|---|---|
| Insert a new managed element outside an opaque range | Ordered anchor remains stable; no affected source reference depends on surrounding positions. | Stop before save if the dependency/anchor cannot be established. |
| Edit text inside known simple text | The complete range and style/annotation mapping is known and UTF-8 boundaries are valid. | Preserve the source; block the intersecting edit/save rather than dropping unknown runs/children. |
| Replace text crossing multiple runs or opaque children | Each run/style/annotation transform and ordering rule is tested. | Safe-stop on the concrete overlap. |
| Change a paragraph role | Exact role mapping exists and source-owned attributes/children, visibility, IDs and references remain attached. | No Version 6 fallback or rewrite; preserve and report the unsupported role. |
| Split/join/reorder paragraphs | ID allocation/retention and all affected anchors, nested scene data and known references have a verified transformation. | Stop if authored nested data would be lost or a dependency ambiguous; do not duplicate or silently delete attachments. |
| Edit/delete a scene heading or alter scene boundaries | SceneProperties and nested beats can be retained/reassociated with verified semantics. | Escalate the concrete authored-data conflict; no inferred destructive consent. |
| Edit script with cross-domain board links | Script and boards remain independent; preserve board objects and their own internal relationships. User explicitly permits cross-domain links to be orphaned silently. | Do not block ordinary script edits solely because a script/board link may be orphaned; do not delete board objects or unrelated authored data. |
| Edit/delete title-page content | Exact source paragraph/run anchor and all title-page formatting/header/footer remain retained; edit UI is not in this order. | Defer title-page editing; do not infer metadata from position. |
| Write after any unsupported/malformed/ambiguous mapping | Complete candidate passes bounded parse, identity/reference and output checks; destination/session transaction is implemented later. | Do not serialize a lossy candidate or claim successful native save. |

## Checks, limitations and handoff

- Structural inspection of both references used a local Python standard-library XML parser and emitted only role labels, section/element counts and attribute names. It did not dereference any recorded local path or print/copy document text or image payload.
- Fixture checks: both synthetic XML files parsed as well-formed XML with Version 6-shaped roots; the preservation fixture contains all 17 distinct requested role labels, a namespace-qualified opaque child, multiple text runs, nested synthetic scene data and synthetic board/item/image identifiers. Assertions also confirmed there is no path attribute or image payload. These are fixture-shape checks only.
- Fresh project checks on this worktree: `cargo fmt --all -- --check` passed; `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets -- -D warnings` passed; `cargo test --workspace` passed (231 tests across workspace targets, 0 failures); `cargo build --workspace` passed; `cargo check --workspace` passed; `git diff --check` passed before staging. The Rust checks establish the unchanged workspace still builds/tests, not FDX behavior.
- Parser-dependency investigation: the first unversioned inverse-dependency query was ambiguous because the lockfile contains quick-xml 0.30.0 and 0.41.0; version-qualified `cargo tree --workspace -i ...` queries completed and reported no active workspace dependency path on this host. The cached 0.41.0 manifest declares MIT and its source exposes namespace-aware event parsing; no direct core dependency was added.
- No Rust code, Cargo manifest, parser, FDX writer, native behavior, or Final Draft check was changed or exercised. No identifiable Final Draft installation/version/platform was exercised or supplied as evidence for a real-app test; no such check is claimed.
- No parser, resource budget, round-trip, ID/reference, role layout, or edit-matrix behavior has been tested by an implementation. The sample count is not a conformance oracle.
- The material mapping blockers are the lack of proven durable homes for ScriptCraft-only identity/schema/profile/policy state, no verified minimal accepted template, and no tested supported encoding/resource limits or safe transformation of all opaque/anchored content. Carry these to WO-002 and the codec orders; do not silently promote the candidates above into guarantees.
- The only safe handoff is to preserve these unresolved findings and let the fresh independent WO-001 validator assess the attributable report/fixtures against the WO. Do not start WO-002 on the assumption that these blockers have been resolved.
